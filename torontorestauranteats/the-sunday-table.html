A neighbourhood welcome

The Sunday Table has the comfortable feel of a neighbourhood regular: soft lighting, a handful of well-spaced tables, and a welcome that feels personal. It works equally well for a weeknight meal or a slower weekend dinner. Nothing about the room asks you to make a performance of being there.

The immediate attraction was the pace. There was time to look through the menu, ask a question, and catch up before thinking about the first course. The smaller dining room gives the evening a sense of intimacy without making the surrounding tables feel too close.

Keep the first course simple

Warm bread is worth ordering straight away. It is the kind of starter that makes a table feel settled, particularly when you are still deciding what to eat. A shared opening dish is plenty before the mains, and it leaves room to enjoy the comforting plates that are the menu’s main draw.

The selection is smaller than at a larger bistro. Rather than seeing that as a limitation, we found it made the decision easier: choose the dishes that sound most appealing and leave the rest for another visit. This is a restaurant that suits a focused order.

Comfort in a bowl

The pasta is generous and unfussy, with enough texture to hold its own against the sauce. The appeal is not a surprising combination or an elaborate finish; it is a well-balanced bowl that stays enjoyable from the first forkful to the last. It fits the room’s relaxed character.

Sharing a starter before a main each feels like the right amount of food. The portions are satisfying, so extras are best considered once you know how hungry the table is. A slower pace helps here: there is no need to make every decision before the first plate arrives.

A comfortable neighbourhood table where attentive service and familiar cooking do the work.

Service and the evening as a whole

The team keeps an eye on the room without hovering. Check-ins arrive when they are useful, and there is space to keep talking without having to pause the conversation for constant interruptions. Our meal moved at a comfortable pace, which contributed as much to the experience as the food.

For value, the strength lies in the whole evening: a welcoming room, fair-feeling portions, and service that makes it easy to relax. Diners looking for a large menu or a showpiece occasion may want something different. For good conversation over a simple dinner, the balance feels right.

The verdict

We would happily return for another ordinary evening made a little better. Come with someone who enjoys an unhurried meal, start with warm bread, and leave time for a proper catch-up. The Sunday Table makes its case quietly, through the comfort of the room and the care of the people running it.
